40+ Communities within Egbema Oil Kingdom

Egbema Oil Kingdom comprises several communities spread across Imo and Rivers states. Each community has its unique cultural identity, traditions, and contributions to the Kingdom.

Other Communities in Egbema Clan Are: Ebocha, Ekpe-Aggah, Ekpe-Mgbede, Ekpe-Etekwuru, Ekpe-Ekugba, Ekpe-Umuorji, Ekpe-Umudike, Ojiji, Ugada-Mmahu, Ugada-Abacheke, Ugada-Okwuzi, Ukwugba, Elua, Nwari, Aliocha I, & II, Nkpataku I, Nkpataku Ishiugwu, Iyioji, Udogalezhi, Omanpe, Ajagwu, Anchor Inn, Nchichiogologo (Location).

Mmahu & Ugada-Mmahu

The administrative and cultural hub of Egbema, known for its oil deposits and vibrant festivals and community events, including fishing activities.

History: Mmahu is one of the oldest communities in Egbema Kingdom, established over 300 years ago by migrant fishermen from the Obufia heartland. The community has preserved its ancestral traditions and played a significant role in the early palm oil and yam trade.

Location: Central Egbema, along the river banks

Population: Approximately 8,500 residents comprises 12 Villages

Traditions: Annual Mmahu Fishing Festival, Masquerade Dance, New Yam Festival

Landmarks: Ancient Mmahu Shrine, Community Square, Mmahu River Port

Ruler: HRH Ikechukwu Cosmos Iwuoha

RulerTitle: Eze Mma I of Mmahu

Culture: The Mmahu people are known for their elaborate masquerade festivals and traditional fishing practices. The community maintains a strong connection to water spirits and celebrates the annual 'Igwaji' festival.

Festivals: Mmahu Fishing Festival (March), New Yam Festival (September), Masquerade Festival (January)

Notable: Home to the oldest known shrine in Egbema Kingdom

Abacheke & Ugada-Abacheke

A community known for its oil deposits and active participation in local governance and traditional hunting.

History: Abacheke was founded in the early 18th century by hunters from the Abacheke clan. The community grew around a central market that became a trading hub for surrounding villages. During the colonial era, Abacheke was known for its resistance to external domination.

Location: Northern Egbema, near the forest reserve

Population: Approximately 6,200 residents, comprises 9 villages

Traditions: Hunting Festival, Abacheke Day, Traditional Wrestling

Landmarks: Central Market Square, Hunting Shrine, Community Hall

Ruler: HRH Chief Sally Ikeji

RulerTitle: Eze of Abacheke

Culture: The community prides itself on its hunting heritage. Young men are trained in traditional hunting techniques and the use of herbal medicines. The 'Ikeji' festival celebrates the hunting spirit.

Festivals: Ikeji Hunting Festival (April), Abacheke Day (October)

Notable: Known for traditional hunting practices and herbal medicine

Notable: Historical trading center with European merchants
